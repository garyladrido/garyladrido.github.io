Why Al Naslaa

One stone. A perfect split. Still standing. The Al Naslaa rock sits about 50 km south of Tayma, Saudi Arabia: a sandstone monolith about 6 metres high and 9 metres wide, cleaved by a joint so straight it looks cut. Each half balances on its own pedestal. Wind and weathering did the work. That is the standard. Two disciplines. One company.

  • Two halves, one stone. Physical build and AI intelligence, split by discipline, united as one company.
  • Engineered precision. The cut is the standard: modular, tender-ready, manufactured accuracy.
  • Built to endure. Mission-critical plant designed for the Gulf, and to run for decades.

The moment

The Gulf is building its digital economy, and AI needs a body. Compute demand is rising while the region pivots from oil to digital. Vision 2030, sovereign-AI mandates, and data-residency rules pull infrastructure onshore. AI is energy and heat constrained. The Gulf has cheap power and the capital to deploy at scale.

Vision 2030 and national-cloud strategies require compute and data in-region.

Control

A factory without an operating system is a building. Layer 05 is plant control: twin, DCIM, BMS, closed-loop setpoints on cooling and power. How Al Naslaa runs what it builds.

Power

Power is the moat. Grid and substation lead times are the bottleneck. Behind-the-meter and hybrid sites: gas + CHP + flare gas for speed-to-power, fuel cells, solar + BESS. SMRs remain a 2030+ option, not a present claim.

Business

Four ways to sell one product family: owned campuses, build-to-suit / leased, embedded whitespace, energy-linked compute. Reference unit economics stay illustrative: $8 to $12M per MW edge, 15 to 25% target IRR, 3 to 5 year payback.
