The engine

When anyone can build, almost nothing is worthy.

Coding is commoditized. An MVP takes hours. Ninety-nine percent of what gets built is noise. Kingmaker is a filter with a factory attached. Internal ideas. Ninety-day kill. Then a chair.

The worthy matrix

Every concept runs these five tests before a line of production code.

1. Proprietary data

If the model can be trained on the public internet, kill it. Fifty other teams will ship it, or the foundation model will eat it in six months. Worthy: sensor logs, pad clocks, claims, closed clinical sets, a docket you can defend.

2. High-consequence workflow

No nice-to-have summarizers. Precision has to matter. A late transformer. A ratepayer spike. A denial that costs a clinic. Hallucination is a dealbreaker, not a vibe.

3. System of record

Stateless prompt boxes have no retention. The software has to sit in the daily job, hold state, and hurt to rip out.

4. Unfair distribution on day one

If sixty percent of capital will go to ads or cold outreach, kill it. Worthy arrives with a buyer, a plant, a roster, or a desk that already says yes.

5. Domain expert as architect

If a generic developer can spec it in an afternoon, it lacks depth. Worthy needs a decade in the trench: how a transformer fails under peak load, how a pad waits on a crane, how a payer actually denies.

How a company is made

  1. A market is named. A buyer, a budget, a workflow that already hurts.
  2. The five filters. Wrapper dies here.
  3. The three gates. Ninety days.
  4. First product inside the studio. Ten conversations, not ten thousand lines.
  5. A chief executive is recruited. Not founder-in-residence theater.
  6. Kingmaker keeps the equity until that chair is filled. Then the operator earns theirs by running it.
