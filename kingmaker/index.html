The three companies The engine

The noun

Venture studio. Builder studio. Not an incubator.

An incubator nurtures other people's companies. An idea lab prototypes and shelves. A venture studio is a factory with a co-founder stake. Kingmaker researches the problem, writes the first product, and recruits the person who will run it. Typical studio equity is thirty to eighty percent until the operator is named. That is the model. That is this company.

AI can code an MVP in an afternoon. That is why the studio is not a coding shop. The scarce thing is a worthy project: proprietary data, a high-consequence workflow, a system of record, unfair distribution, and a domain expert who has been in the trench for a decade. The engine is how we refuse the rest.

Red Rock Ventures writes checks into other people. DCB Sports holds the sports book. Renewable Advisors is the craft. CreatorFi lives in the creator economy. Al Naslaa is its own Gulf company. Kingmaker is where a new operating company is born when Gary has a thesis and no one yet to run it.

What AI changed

Intelligence got cheap. The bottlenecks did not.

AI is a catalyst kingmaker. It destroys the cost of software, drafts, and first prototypes. It does not destroy liability, distribution, power, water, labor, or a hospital's sales cycle. Specialized studios are access kingmakers. They hold the inputs that still take years: compute, closed data, a buyer, a permit, a pad, a clinician who will put their name on it.

Kingmaker is the second kind. We do not compete with OpenAI. We do not wrap a frontier model and call it a company. We put intelligence onto a job that already hurts, then hire the operator who already knows that job.
